# P12_FJ S3 recreation: decisions (written before any comparison with claims values)

**Independence level: FULL.** Inputs: the two raw transcription files only (`New 10 towns 1915-1920.xlsx`, `2020-05 JB Sea Point 1915-1920.xlsx`), plus `Phillips Table 13_all.xlsx` for the Phillips columns of Table 1. The cleaned `POCQMK ... CLEAN` file, the SA_1918 shapefile and the census workbooks were not used. All coding (race, occupation, birthplace, doctor, DiF, influenza) is mine. No `.R`/`.do` originals, `repro_original/`, `compare/`, `audit.md` or other P## folders were opened.

**Subsample.** 31,327 certificates dated 1915-1920 from 11 districts (10 towns + Sea Point 343 rows). Fraserburg, Riversdale, Stellenbosch, Stockenstrom are not recovered: their certificate cells in T1/T2 are `unavailable`; the paper's 39,482 is not reachable (N1 = 30,420 with known race and doctor; N2/N3 = 26,823 complete cases). Every number is a subsample result; the N is in `results_r.csv`.

## Choices and ambiguities
1. **Pandemic** = date 1 Oct 1918 to 31 Jan 1919. Missing day set to 15; days capped at 28 (dates used only at day resolution for the 7-day rule, so up to 3 days' error for late-month deaths). Pre-pandemic (T2/T3/T4) = Jan 1915 to Sep 1918.
2. **Race**: keyword mapping of transcribed labels to White / Coloured (Mixed, Hottentot, Coloured, Malay, Griqua, Gemengd) / Black (Kaffir, Fingo, Xosa, Bechuana and other Bantu labels, Native). Indian, blank and unmatched labels (907 rows) are dropped from race-specific results.
3. **Doctor named**: `Medical man's name` not blank/None/Nil/-/./No doctor/etc. after punctuation stripping. Any other text counts as a named doctor, including initials-only entries.
4. **Signature**: "Yes" = 1; "No", "Mark", "Merk" etc. = 0; other = missing.
5. **Age groups** 0-4, 5-9, 10-14, 15-19, 20-24, 25-34 (ref), 35-49, 50-69, 70+; ages in months/weeks/days set to 0.
6. **Occupation** (Farming = reference): own keyword classification into Professionals/managers, Sales/service (incl. housewife and domestic service), Production (incl. general labourer, railway, miners), Children/pensioners (scholars, pensioners, and blank occupation with age <15 or >=65). Unclassified occupations are missing and drop out of specs 2-3. The paper's HISCO-based coding is unknown to me, so group boundaries differ.
7. **Birthplace**: Cape (reference; the residual, incl. Transkei, Bechuanaland), Africa (outside SA), Asia, Europe, South Africa (Transvaal/OFS/Natal). Blank/unknown = missing (dropped in specs 2-3).
8. **Death in family** = another certificate in the same town with the same surname dated 0-7 days earlier (nearest preceding record). Blank surname = 0.
9. **Nurse** (occupation contains "nurse", excluding wet nurse); **Railway** (railway, platelayer, locomotive, engine driver, shunter, ganger, signalman); **Street/road/stad** and **Farm** are defined on usual place of residence text (the paper's source variable for Farm is ambiguous; spec text says "farm dummy" among location variables).
10. **Table 1**: certificate flu deaths = pandemic-window certificates whose cause of death contains influenza/influenza-spelling variants/"spanish"/"grippe"/"flu". Pneumonia alone is excluded; "pneumonia following influenza" is included via "influenz". Phillips columns are read from Table 13 (exact district name match; Elliot vs Elliotdale handled). Rates use the **Phillips total population** as denominator for both sources (the paper states no denominator; its own two rates imply different ones).
11. **Table 4**: modified Whipple = 100 x sum_d |p_d - 0.1| / 1.8 over terminal digits 0-9 of the number as written in "duration of last illness" (units ignored, no age-heaping correction). Doctors matched by surname in `Medical man's name` (Heynes matched as "heyn"; "Don" as a whole word). Cells with fewer than 5 observations are missing. Index definition is my reading of "0 = equal distribution, 100 = one digit".
12. **Table 5**: pooled logit (`fixest::feglm`, binomial) with district dummies (`factor(town)`), so an Intercept exists but depends on the reference district (Cradock here; unknown in the paper). `se` in results_r.csv = **district-clustered** (the paper's stated SE; 11 clusters, so the VCOV is fragile and fixest warned it was not PSD). Conventional (iid) SEs are in `output/table5_all_se_subsample.csv`. Spec 1 sample = known race and doctor; specs 2-3 share one complete-case sample (as in the paper). Fit statistics: pseudo-R2 = 1 - ll/ll0, BIC from fixest, squared correlation of fitted vs observed.
13. **Predicted probabilities (TXT_p9..p15)**: from spec 3, Paarl dummy, reference person (white, 25-34, female, farming, Cape-born, illiterate informant, all other dummies 0). Pandemic = 0 unless stated; for TXT_p10, p12, p13 I use pandemic = 1 because the text does not say (guess, not tuned). TXT_p9_j = p9_i - p9_d. TXT_p14_a = difference of median death dates (days), railway minus others, within the pandemic window. TXT_p16_a/b = Black coefficient in spec 2 / spec 3.
14. **Not attempted (unavailable)**: TXT_p1_a (6% mortality), p2_a/b (Phillips national totals), p2_e/f/g (census shares; Census 1911.xlsx layout not parsed within budget), p3_a (Transkei, Fig. 1), p4_a/b (Kimberley), p7_a/b (Fig. 5 child mortality), p15, p16 where the quantity is not a model output; all cells in unrecovered districts.

## Post-hoc changes
- After the first run, Table 1 Elliot failed to match Phillips (two rows begin "Elli"); changed to exact-name matching. No other change was made after seeing results, and no change was informed by claims values.
- No collinearity or separation checks beyond fixest's own handling were run; some terms may be unstable in the 343-row Sea Point and small cells.
